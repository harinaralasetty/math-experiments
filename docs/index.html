AI Agents Find 1,350 New Terms for Open Math Sequences

By Hari Naralasetty (GitHub · LinkedIn) · 2026-10-22

Working with AI agents, Hari Naralasetty computed 1,350 new terms for 10 integer sequences in the OEIS that were marked as needing more terms.

Each new term was produced by two independent programs that agree exactly. The same process made partial progress on the Busy Beaver problem BB(6) and found no new records in three record-search batches.

Method

This work belongs to experimental mathematics: using computation to study mathematical questions. AI agents searched for candidates and wrote independent checkers. The published status of each problem was checked before any search, and a result is reported only after its checks were re-run from scratch. A long computation, or a checker that accepts malformed input, does not count as progress.

What these results mean

The new OEIS terms are exact values beyond what was published, computed twice by different programs. The Busy Beaver work proves facts about parts of how two machines behave, but not yet whether either machine ever stops. The record searches matched the best known answers without beating them; that does not show the known answers are the best possible.

Published status is a dated source-search snapshot, with no coverage of unpublished work. See verification commands and limitations.

Questions and answers

What was found?

The work added 1,350 new terms to 10 OEIS sequences, made partial progress on BB(6), and found no new records in the three record-search batches.

Were the results checked?

Yes. Two independent programs agree exactly on every new OEIS term. Separate checkers verify the Busy Beaver local rules and the saved record-search constructions.

Are the terms on the OEIS yet?

The new terms have not yet been submitted to the OEIS. The downloads here include the published overlap and the computed extensions.

Did the agents solve BB(6)?

No. The work checked partial results for two candidate machines, but neither has a new halting or non-halting decision.

What is the OEIS?

The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences is a reference collection of integer sequences, with definitions, known terms, references and programs.

Can I re-run the checks?

Yes. Run make verify-site and make verify-math from the repository root. The reproduction page explains the dependencies and the scope of each check.
